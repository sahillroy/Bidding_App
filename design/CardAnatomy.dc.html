The staggering is the whole trick. Move all eight on one duration and the hover reads as a switch flipping. Let the border snap, the brackets draw, the card tilt under the cursor, the art drift against it, and the light sweep last — each finishing at a different moment — and it reads as a physical object responding to a hand.

The sweep fires once per hover and does not repeat. A looping shimmer reads as a skeleton loader — it tells the eye something is still arriving, which is exactly wrong on content that has already loaded.

Turns blue
The 1px border, the outer glow, the wash rising off the bottom of the image, and the revealed Place bid → row.
Stays put
Price, bid count and countdown never move or recolour on hover — they are the facts you are scanning for, and they must not shift under the cursor.
What the cursor tracking costs
Tilt, spotlight and parallax need the pointer position, so that part is JS — the only JS in the whole direction. One listener per card, coalesced into a single rAF, writing four CSS variables. The rect is measured once on pointerenter, never in the move handler, so there is no layout thrash. It runs on the hovered card alone; the other 40 are idle.
Reduced motion
Every duration collapses to 1ms and both transforms drop. Colour still changes, so the card is still obviously interactive — the affordance survives, the movement does not.
Keyboard
The card is an <a>, so it is tabbable for free. :focus-visible paints the same blue as a 2px ring at 3px offset — a keyboard user sees exactly what a mouse user sees, which is the point.
